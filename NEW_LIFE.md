# New Life — notice the pattern, choose a next step

The purpose of Addiction Breaker is to give people a readable account of their own gambling patterns and somewhere to write what is happening around them. A wake-up call should be grounded in evidence and followed by something practical the person can do.

The tool stands alone. It can later join a larger project, but it must never depend on that project for check-ins, Echoes or access to support.

## 1. Show what the records support

Show recorded stakes, returns, and the difference. Show how many recorded losses were immediately followed by another bet within 30 seconds, with the denominator alongside the percentage. Explain omissions and uncertainty before presenting conclusions.

Historical return is `recorded returns / recorded stakes`. It describes the uploaded history. It does not tell us what the next session will return or the probability of recovering a loss. No amount of rhetoric can supply a probability model that the data does not contain.

The Hook Score is an experimental weighted index: chase gap 30%, stake escalation 25%, acceleration 20%, UTC night share 15%, and near-miss continuation 10%. It was developed using synthetic examples. Neither its weights nor its thresholds have been clinically validated. It must never label someone addicted, recovered, safe, or beyond help.

Calendar days 1–3 and 28–31 are simply calendar windows. They are not evidence of someone's payday. Night hours use UTC. A pattern in a log cannot prove that a platform knew someone's state of mind or targeted them personally.

## 2. Give observation somewhere to go

A check-in records urge, loneliness, meaningful contact, rest, money pressure, whether gambling occurred and the person's own note. It is an invitation to notice, not a test to pass.

Several high-pressure ratings can prompt a pause or a support link. The app does not predict relapse and does not interpret a missing entry as a good or bad day. Seven-day averages use only recorded entries in that calendar window.

There is no instruction to keep gambling in order to collect data. Someone can stop, seek help or start a journal immediately. Uploading gambling history is optional; the journal works without it.

## 3. Echoes preserve the person's voice

An Echo is an exact note from a strictly earlier date when the person explicitly reported not gambling and rated urge at least 6/10. Notes are ranked by similarity in urge, loneliness and money pressure.

This is a retrieval rule, not a clinical threshold. The app never fabricates a memory, rewrites the note, treats an unknown answer as abstinence, or uses a future-dated entry. When no suitable note exists, it says so.

## 4. Make the next action small and available

The person can choose a ten-minute pause, get a drink of water, move to another room, message someone, take a comfortable walk, do a small task, or use their own written plan. The timer gives that choice a boundary. It does not promise that an urge will end after ten minutes.

A personal plan can name one action, one person to contact, and one thing the person wants to protect. Longer-term routines and professional support can sit alongside this tool. The application does not prescribe an eight-week treatment course or use a numerical score to decide whether someone needs help.

If gambling happens again, the person can record it without losing access, being shamed, or resetting an achievement system. An unchanged Hook Score cannot establish that an episode was harmless or isolated.

## 5. Respect the data

No account is required. Journal entries and plans remain in the browser. Persistence is optional and explicitly described as unencrypted. Users can export, restore and erase. No inference is sent to an AI provider. Selected files are analysed in a browser Web Worker; their contents are not uploaded.

Privacy claims must include practical limits: shared browser profiles, downloaded backups, browser-data clearing and host infrastructure all matter. There is no hidden sync or silent contact with family or support services.

## 6. Keep support one step away

The application links to NHS information and GamCare's current contact options, including text-based routes. Support does not depend on an upload or a score. It also points to practical money advice. This application is not medical treatment, a crisis service, or a substitute for professional care.

## What success means for this release

The person can open the app, understand where their data goes, write and retrieve a check-in, see their own earlier words, choose a next action and access support. If they upload a history, the app reports supported counts and admits what is unknown.

Whether the tool improves outcomes is a separate question requiring real evaluation. Synthetic demonstrations and passing software tests do not answer it.

**Confront the pattern honestly. Leave the person room to act.**
